\begin{abstract}
When must an operator-valued obstruction associated with an
involutive system vanish?  This paper answers that question inside
the Six Birds framework by naming Self-Dual Trace Confinement
(SDTC) as the recognition source for formed closures with genuine
involutive self-duality.  The mechanism is the
duality-confinement membrane theorem: for an involutive object
ledger with separating anti-invariant readout, a sequence of
positive domination records \(\AX\preceq B_n\) with
\(\trace B_n\to0\) forces the anti-invariant ledger \(\AX\) to
vanish and confines visible mass to the fixed locus of the
duality.  The supporting apparatus develops the involutive object
ledger, the anti-invariant ledger and trace identity, direct
qualitative and quantitative confinement, completed-domination
bridges, Douglas factorization in typed-cone form, exhaustive
moving ledgers, and optimized scalar trace budgets.
Operator-theoretic realization over trace-class operators is an
open extension, as is a derivation of the AM--GM step inside a
typed real-arithmetic structure.  The development is formalized in
Lean as a mathlib-free mechanization at the typed positive-cone
level (Appendix~\ref{app:formalization}).  Cross-substrate
generalizations such as CPT symmetry,
gauge invariance, quantum self-adjointness, and function-field RH
are presented as structural predictions, not theorems; the paper
develops the abstract apparatus layer and names the hypotheses any
single-substrate validation must supply.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

When must an operator-valued obstruction associated with an
involutive system vanish?  The simplest form of the question is
familiar.  Suppose a space of objects carries an involution \(J\),
so that applying \(J\) twice returns the original object, and
suppose the objects are observed through a Hilbert-valued readout
\(\psi\) whose response space carries a compatible isometric
involution \(\Jiso\).  The response then splits into invariant and
anti-invariant parts.  The anti-invariant projector is
\[
  \Pminus = \frac{I_Y-\Jiso}{2},
\]
and the corresponding anti-invariant readout is
\[
  \psim(x) = \Pminus \psi(x).
\]
This readout detects the part of the object that changes sign under
the duality.  If its vanishing set is exactly the fixed locus
\(\Fix(J)\), then showing \(\psim=0\) in the appropriate measured
sense is the same as showing that visible mass is confined to
\(\Fix(J)\).

The obstruction studied in this paper is the positive operator
formed from that anti-invariant readout,
\[
  \AX = \int_X \psim(x)\psim(x)^*\,d\mu(x).
\]
In classical analytic language one would place \(\AX\) in a positive
trace-class cone and use the trace identity
\[
  \trace \AX = \int_X \|\psim(x)\|^2\,d\mu(x),
\]
so \(\AX\) vanishes precisely when the anti-invariant readout
vanishes almost everywhere, modulo the usual positivity hypotheses.
The abstract argument is an operator squeeze: if there is a sequence
of positive bounds \(B_n\) with
\[
  \AX \preceq B_n
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \trace B_n \to 0,
\]
then monotonicity of the trace gives \(0 \leq \trace \AX \leq
\trace B_n\) for all \(n\), hence \(\trace \AX=0\), and positivity
forces \(\AX=0\).  Separation of the readout then converts
\(\AX=0\) into the confinement statement
\[
  \mu\bigl(X\setminus \Fix(J)\bigr)=0.
\]
The remaining sections make each part of this preview precise:
the involutive object ledger in \Cref{sec:involutive_ledger}, the
anti-invariant ledger and separating readout in
\Cref{sec:anti_invariant_ledger}, the direct confinement results in
\Cref{sec:direct_confinement}, and the full squeeze theorem in
\Cref{sec:master_theorem}.

The Six Birds framework gives this argument a structural
interpretation.  Six Birds Theory is used here as a typed, audited
calculus for naming structural data in a formed closure: a typed
presentation of a mathematical substrate together with the assumption
that its carriers, observables, comparison maps, and audit data have
been admissibly closed under the framework's formation discipline.
Within that vocabulary, the tuple
\[
  \IOL=(X,J,\mu,\psi,Y,\Jiso)
\]
is called an involutive object ledger.  In ordinary terms, it is a
measure space or finite weighted carrier with an involution, together
with an equivariant Hilbert-valued observable.  The paper's central
structural assertion is named Self-Dual Trace Confinement,
\(\GamSDTC\), abbreviated SDTC: a formed closure with genuine
involutive self-duality and a
separating anti-invariant readout cannot host visible mass away from
the fixed locus of that duality.

SDTC is named as a recognition source: an explicit structural
hypothesis about the substrate, supplied as content of the formed
layer rather than proved from earlier framework primitives.  The
paper plays two roles.  It states the SDTC structural law and the
closure shape in which it applies, and it proves the
duality-confinement membrane theorem: once domination records
\(\AX \preceq B_n\) with \(\trace B_n\to0\) are available,
fixed-locus confinement follows from the typed positive-cone
squeeze sketched above.

A useful mental model is the functional-equation symmetry of the
Riemann zeta function.  The involution
\[
  J_L(s)=1-\bar{s}
\]
on the complex plane has critical-line fixed locus
\(\Re(s)=1/2\), and the signed real-part displacement
\(\Re(s)-1/2\) is its anti-invariant coordinate.  The example serves
as a guide to the shape of the abstraction---an involution, a
fixed locus, an anti-invariant readout, and a positive ledger
measuring mass away from the fixed locus.  No result about
\(\zeta\) is used below.

The typed cone records only what the squeeze proof needs: the order
\(\preceq\), the trace functional \(\trace\), trace monotonicity,
and the principle that a positive element of trace zero is the zero
element.  A full operator-theoretic realization over trace-class
operators, Hilbert--Schmidt operators, Schatten ideals, or the
trace-norm topology \citep{Simon2005,ReedSimon1980} is not developed
here; extending the theorem to that operator-theoretic setting is an
open extension.

The classical ingredients in the apparatus---Loewner order
\citep{Loewner1934,Bhatia1997}, trace functionals
\citep{Simon2005}, Markov-style estimates \citep{Folland1999},
AM--GM optimization \citep{HardyLittlewoodPolya1952}, and Douglas
factorization \citep{Douglas1966}---are not claimed as novel.  What
is new is the structural packaging: the anti-invariant ledger, the
domination-record discipline, and the master theorem that converts
vanishing trace budgets into fixed-locus confinement.  The optimized
scalar trace budget in \Cref{sec:exhaustive_squeeze_and_budgets}
takes the AM--GM step as a typed-scalar hypothesis; a substantive
derivation inside the abstract scalar encoding would require
additional typed real-arithmetic structure.

Cross-substrate examples such as CPT symmetry, gauge invariance,
particle--antiparticle duality, quantum self-adjointness, and
function-field analogues are structural predictions and validation
targets for future work, not theorems established here.
 

\section{Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

The framework context for SDTC is the Six Birds organization of
formed-layer structural laws.  In this paper, Six Birds Theory is
used as a typed and audited calculus for the closure-formation
discipline originating in Foundations I: it gives names to the
carriers, observables, comparison maps, and audit data that a
mathematical substrate presents when treated as a formed object.
A \emph{formed closure} is such a typed bundle together with the
assumption that the bundle has been closed under the relevant
formation discipline.  Concretely, one may think of a
substrate---algebraic, analytic, computational, or
geometric---equipped with a finite amount of visible structure and
with a declaration that this visible structure is stable under the
closure rules being used.  The admissibility schemas and typed
record interfaces used for this purpose are those of Foundations II
\cite{TsiokosFoundationsII2026}; the audited vocabulary of
visibility, threshold, gate, and claim-status data is part of the
Foundations III layer \cite{TsiokosFoundationsIII2026}.

This language does not replace the underlying mathematics; it
records which data are visible at a given layer and which comparison
principles are licensed there.  When the paper later writes an
involutive ledger \(\IOL\), a positive cone containing \(\AX\), or a
domination sequence \(B_n\), these objects do not add analytic
content.  They make explicit which carrier, readout, order relation,
trace functional, and audit conditions are in play.  The local
mathematics is the operator-style squeeze of the introduction; the
typed framework fixes the bookkeeping that keeps the squeeze inside
the same formed layer throughout.

Closure content is treated as a structural fact of the formed layer
rather than something to be re-derived from earlier framework
primitives.  If a witness, comparison map, domination sequence, or
recognition record is part of what makes the layer formed, the
structural law is allowed to name that item as supplied data.  This
is not an axiom inserted into the surrounding metatheory; it
identifies which hypothesis about the substrate is load-bearing.

The framework distinguishes a structural law from an apparatus
theorem.  The structural law names the formed-layer recognition
content; here \(\GamSDTC\) says that an admissibly formed involutive
self-dual closure supplies the trace-vanishing domination data
needed by the confinement mechanism.  The apparatus theorem is the
ordinary mathematical implication proved once those data are
present: a separating anti-invariant readout and bounds
\(\AX \preceq B_n\) with \(\trace B_n\to0\) force confinement on
\(\Fix(J)\).

The V-Differential classification is the framework's way of sorting
structural laws by the determining-state content the formed layer
permits.  Here a determining state is the internal state through
which later observable behavior is required to factor.  In some
substrates that state may expose witness-like data: for example,
explicit configurations, certificates, or geometric carriers.  In a
\emph{trace-state-only} column, by contrast, the admissible
determining-state information is restricted to trace observables on
a typed positive cone.  The readout is allowed to see aggregate
quantities such as \(\trace A\), but not a full state vector, a
hidden witness, or a richer invariant that would determine the
answer directly.

SDTC is the V-Differential trace-state-only law for involutive
self-dual closures.  Its visible object is not a hidden witness for
membership in \(\Fix(J)\); it is the anti-invariant ledger \(\AX\)
and its trace budget.  The readout \(\psim\) detects displacement
from the fixed locus, but the formed layer's determining information
is the positive-cone trace state: \(\trace \AX\), domination
inequalities, and vanishing bounds.  This is why the trace identity
and monotonicity of \(\trace\) are central.  The proof never needs
access to a denser internal state once the trace-state domination
records have been supplied.

Within this framing, SDTC joins parallel internal Six Birds
developments that organize other formed closures along the same
shape: a recognition source names the formed-layer structural
content, and an apparatus theorem extracts the advertised
mathematical consequence once that content is present.  Those
developments are mentioned here only to position SDTC inside the
program; none is an input to any definition, estimate, or theorem
below.

This section is organizational rather than deductive.  The
mathematical work begins in the next section: defining \(\IOL\),
constructing \(\AX\), stating the separation and domination
hypotheses, and proving the master theorem.
 

\section{Involutive object ledger}
\label{sec:involutive_ledger}

The first piece of apparatus is the object on which the confinement
argument is allowed to operate.  The framework section explained
that SDTC is a structural law over a formed layer.  For the present
paper, the mathematical data inside such a layer are concrete: a
carrier of visible objects, an involution on that carrier, a
positive measure or finite weight system, and a Hilbert-valued
observable compatible with the involution.  Throughout, this package
is denoted
\[
  \IOL=(X,J,\mu,\psi,Y,\Jiso).
\]
Read first as ordinary analysis: \(X\) is the space whose mass we
want to localize; \(J:X\to X\) is a symmetry with
\(J^2=\mathrm{id}_X\); \(\mu\) says which objects are visible;
\(Y\) is the response space in which the objects are observed;
\(\Jiso:Y\to Y\) is the corresponding symmetry of responses; and
\(\psi:X\to Y\) is the observable.

\begin{definition}[Involutive object ledger\protect\footnote{Realized in Lean as \nolinkurl{Involution.InvolutiveObjectLedger}; see App~\ref{app:formalization}.}]
\label{def:duality_confinement:involutive-object-ledger}
An \emph{involutive object ledger} is a tuple
\[
  \IOL=(X,J,\mu,\psi,Y,\Jiso)
\]
consisting of the following data:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a visible carrier \(X\);
  \item an involution \(J:X\to X\), so \(J(Jx)=x\) for every
    \(x\in X\);
  \item a positive measure, or in the finite case a positive weight
    system, \(\mu\) on \(X\);
  \item a real Hilbert response space \(Y\);
  \item a linear isometric involution \(\Jiso:Y\to Y\);
  \item a measurable readout \(\psi:X\to Y\) satisfying the
    equivariance condition
    \[
      \psi(Jx)=\Jiso\psi(x)
      \qquad (x\in X).
    \]
\end{enumerate}
The fixed locus of the ledger involution is
\[
  \Fix(J)=\{x\in X: Jx=x\}.
\]
\end{definition}

The definition separates carrier-side symmetry from response-side
symmetry: \(J\) acts on the objects whose mass is being studied,
while \(\Jiso\) acts on the Hilbert space in which those objects
are read.  The equivariance condition says that observing an
object after applying the carrier involution is the same as
observing first and then applying the response involution.  Here
``linear isometric involution'' for \(\Jiso\) means
\[
  \Jiso^2=I_Y,
  \qquad
  \langle \Jiso v,\Jiso w\rangle_Y=\langle v,w\rangle_Y
  \quad(v,w\in Y).
\]
In topological or measure-theoretic examples the displayed maps are
normally assumed measurable, and often \(\mu\) is \(J\)-invariant
or at least \(J\)-quasi-invariant.  The abstract ledger definition
records only the positive measured carrier and the equivariant
readout; later hypotheses, such as separation and trace-class
positivity, state the additional structure needed for a particular
confinement argument.

The convention covers both finite and analytic situations.  In a
finite ledger, \(X\) may be a finite list of roots, defects, modes,
or objects, and \(\mu(x)>0\) is the weight assigned to an object
visible to the argument.  In a continuous ledger, \(\mu\) is a
positive measure on the carrier, and the statements are read modulo
null sets where appropriate.  The phrase ``visible support'' always
means the part of \(X\) carrying positive \(\mu\)-mass:
positive-weight points in the finite case and non-null measurable
regions in the measure-theoretic case.  This is the only part of
the carrier that the confinement theorem can see; objects outside
the visible support may exist as elements of \(X\), but they play
no role in the measure conclusion
\(\mu(X\setminus\Fix(J))=0\).

The fixed locus \(\Fix(J)\) is the target of the confinement
conclusion.  In a topological example it is usually a closed
subspace, but no topology is needed to name it: it is the set of
points left unchanged by \(J\).  The ledger terminology
emphasizes that \(\mu\) is not merely background measure; it is the
accounting device that tells the theorem which visible objects
remain after the obstruction has been squeezed to zero.  The
anti-invariant projection associated with \(\Jiso\) and the positive
ledger built from it are introduced in
\Cref{sec:anti_invariant_ledger}.

A simple model is complex conjugation.  Let \(X\) be a bounded
measurable region in \(\mathbb{C}\), stable under conjugation, with
\(\mu\) the restricted planar Lebesgue measure.  Put
\[
  J(z)=\overline{z},\qquad
  Y=\mathbb{R}^2,\qquad
  \Jiso(u,v)=(u,-v),
\]
and let
\[
  \psi(z)=(\operatorname{Re}z,\operatorname{Im}z).
\]
Then
\[
  \psi(Jz)
  =
  (\operatorname{Re}z,-\operatorname{Im}z)
  =
  \Jiso\psi(z),
\]
so the readout is equivariant.  The fixed locus is the real axis
inside \(X\); the coordinate that changes sign is the imaginary
coordinate, so mass away from the real axis is exactly the mass
visible to the sign-changing part of the readout.  The toy model
contains no analysis, but it displays the geometry of the
definition: a duality, a fixed locus, and an observable whose
coordinates respect the duality.

The same pattern explains the motivating analytic example.  In the
functional-equation symmetry of the completed Riemann zeta function
\citep{TitchmarshHeathBrown1986}, the involution
\[
  J_L(s)=1-\overline{s}
\]
on the critical strip has fixed locus the critical line
\(\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2\).  Specializing \(X\) to the visible
multiset of nontrivial zeros with \(\mu\) the multiplicity-weighted
measure makes \(\operatorname{Re}(s)-1/2\) the sign-changing
displacement from the fixed line.  The worked single-substrate
validation of that instance is developed in
\citet{TsiokosRHviaSDTC2026}; no result from there is used here.

The mathematical work begins once this ledger is equipped with its
anti-invariant projection, trace ledger, and separation hypotheses.
 

\section{Anti-invariant ledger and separation}
\label{sec:anti_invariant_ledger}

Fix an involutive object ledger
\(\IOL=(X,J,\mu,\psi,Y,\Jiso)\).  The preceding section supplied
the carrier-side involution \(J\) and the response-side involution
\(\Jiso\).  This section extracts the part of the readout that
changes sign under the duality and turns it into a positive
observable.  The isometric involution \(\Jiso\) splits \(Y\) into
invariant and anti-invariant directions, and the confinement
mechanism reads only the second component: the direction in which
the response is reversed by the involution.

The anti-invariant projector is
\[
  \Pminus=\frac{I_Y-\Jiso}{2}.
\]
Since \(\Jiso^2=I_Y\), this is the orthogonal projection onto the
subspace of vectors \(v\in Y\) satisfying \(\Jiso v=-v\).  Applying
it to the ledger readout gives the anti-invariant readout
\[
  \psim(x)=\Pminus\psi(x).
\]
Equivariance of \(\psi\) makes this readout odd with respect to the
carrier involution:
\[
  \psim(Jx)
  =
  \Pminus\psi(Jx)
  =
  \Pminus\Jiso\psi(x)
  =
  -\Pminus\psi(x)
  =
  -\psim(x).
\]
So \(\psim\) is the component of the observable that can detect
departure from the fixed locus.  If \(x\in\Fix(J)\), the same
identity gives \(\psim(x)=-\psim(x)\), hence \(\psim(x)=0\) over a
real response space.  The converse need not hold automatically; it
is exactly the separation condition defined below.

The positive object associated with \(\psim\) is introduced in a
typed positive cone rather than as a trace-class operator: an
ordered setting with a positive order \(\preceq\), a trace
functional \(\trace\), and two positivity principles.  Trace is
monotone,
\[
  A\preceq B \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \trace A\leq \trace B,
\]
and a positive element of trace zero is the zero element.  The
classical model is the cone of positive trace-class operators on a
Hilbert space; the typed abstraction records only the order, trace,
and positivity facts used by the confinement proof.

\begin{definition}[Anti-invariant object ledger\protect\footnote{Realized in Lean as \nolinkurl{AntiInvariantLedger.AntiInvariantLedger}; see App~\ref{app:formalization}.}]
\label{def:duality_confinement:anti-invariant-ledger}
The \emph{anti-invariant object ledger} of \(\IOL\) is the positive
cone element
\[
  \AX
  :=
  \int_X \psim(x)\psim(x)^*\,d\mu(x),
  \qquad
  \psim(x)=\Pminus\psi(x).
\]
Equivalently, \(\AX\) is the Gram-type positive element generated
by integrating the anti-invariant readout against the object-ledger
measure \(\mu\).
\end{definition}

In a finite weighted ledger, the same formula reads
\[
  \AX=\sum_{x\in X}\mu(x)\,\psim(x)\psim(x)^*.
\]
This finite expression is the easiest way to see positivity: every
summand is a rank-one positive contribution from the part of the
readout that changes sign under the involution.  In a
measure-theoretic ledger, the integral notation records the
corresponding positive accumulation over the visible support of
\(\mu\).

In the classical trace-class reading the displayed integral is a
positive operator on \(Y\) assembled from rank-one positive
operators \(\psim(x)\psim(x)^*\).  In the typed-cone reading the
integral notation specifies the positive element \(\AX\) together
with the trace and order structure needed to compare \(\AX\) with
later domination bounds.  The abstraction preserves the algebra of
positive operators that enters the master theorem without claiming a
development of the full analytic theory of trace-class operators.

\begin{definition}[Separating anti-invariant readout]
\label{def:duality_confinement:separating-readout}
The anti-invariant readout \(\psim\) is
\emph{qualitatively separating} when, on the visible support of
\(\mu\),
\[
  \psim(x)=0
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  x\in\Fix(J).
\]
In the finite weighted case this means the equivalence holds for
every \(x\in X\) with positive weight.  In the measure-theoretic
case it means the equivalence holds outside a \(\mu\)-null set.

It is \emph{quantitatively separating} with modulus \(m\) when
\[
  m:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
\]
and, for every \(\varepsilon>0\) and every \(x\in X\),
\[
  \dist(x,\Fix(J))\geq\varepsilon
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \|\psim(x)\|\geq m(\varepsilon).
\]
\end{definition}

Qualitative separation says that vanishing of the anti-invariant
readout identifies the fixed locus.  Quantitative separation says
more: once a point is at least \(\varepsilon\) away from
\(\Fix(J)\), the anti-invariant readout is bounded below by a
positive amount depending only on that distance scale.  The modulus
is often chosen nondecreasing in applications, but the confinement
estimate only needs the displayed positive lower bound.

The next statement is the trace identity relating \(\trace\AX\) to
the integral of \(\|\psim(x)\|^2\); together with separation, it is
what turns vanishing of \(\AX\) into fixed-locus confinement.

\begin{lemma}[Trace identity\protect\footnote{Verified in Lean as \nolinkurl{AntiInvariantLedger.traceIdentity} via constructor unfolding from the typed \nolinkurl{AntiInvariantLedger} field; see App~\ref{app:formalization}.}]
\label{lem:duality_confinement:trace-identity}
Let \(\AX\) be the anti-invariant ledger of an involutive object
ledger \(\IOL\), with anti-invariant readout \(\psim\).  Then
\[
  \trace\AX
  =
  \int_X \|\psim(x)\|^2\,d\mu(x).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In the classical operator-theoretic regime, where
\[
  \AX=\int_X \psim(x)\psim(x)^*\,d\mu(x)
\]
is a positive trace-class operator on \(Y\), the identity is the
standard rank-one trace computation \citep{ReedSimon1980,Simon2005}.
Taking the trace of each rank-one operator
\(\psim(x)\psim(x)^*\) gives \(\|\psim(x)\|^2\), and integration
against the positive measure \(\mu\) gives the displayed identity.
The right-hand side accordingly measures the mean-square amplitude of
the anti-invariant readout.

In the typed positive cone, the same identity is recorded as a
structural axiom of the cone rather than derived: the cone is
specified by the order, trace, and positivity facts used by the
confinement proof, and the value of \(\trace\) on the generator
\(\int_X\psim(x)\psim(x)^*\,d\mu(x)\) is one of those facts.  The
lemma packages that axiom for use alongside the classical
operator-theoretic derivation.  Either way, the identity holds.
\end{proof}

Vanishing of \(\trace\AX\), vanishing of \(\AX\), and vanishing of
\(\psim\) almost everywhere are therefore equivalent under the
trace identity and the positivity axioms of the cone.  Combining
this with a separating readout is the direct route to the
fixed-locus confinement statement proved in the next section.
 

\section{Direct confinement}
\label{sec:direct_confinement}

With the trace identity in place, the separating-readout conditions
give two confinement consequences.  The first is qualitative: if
the anti-invariant ledger vanishes, then visible mass cannot remain
away from the fixed locus.  The second is quantitative: if the
readout grows at least by a modulus away from \(\Fix(J)\), then the
trace of \(\AX\) bounds the amount of visible mass at that distance
scale.  Together these furnish the consequence step used later by the
master theorem, which adds the separate squeeze argument that forces
\(\AX=0\).

\begin{theorem}[Separation--confinement\protect\footnote{Verified in Lean as \nolinkurl{DirectConfinement.separationConfinement} via typed-bridge composition over the measure-reading bridges \nolinkurl{mu_zero_of_ae} and \nolinkurl{visible_zero_of_ae}; see App~\ref{app:formalization}.}]
\label{thm:duality_confinement:separation-confinement}
Let \(\IOL\) be an involutive object ledger with anti-invariant
ledger \(\AX\) and qualitatively separating anti-invariant readout
\(\psim\).  If \(\AX=0\) in the typed positive cone, then
\[
  \mu\bigl(X\setminus\Fix(J)\bigr)=0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{lem:duality_confinement:trace-identity},
\[
  \trace\AX=\int_X\|\psim(x)\|^2\,d\mu(x).
\]
Since \(\AX=0\), the left-hand side is zero.  The integrand on the
right is nonnegative, so the integral can vanish only when
\(\|\psim(x)\|=0\) for \(\mu\)-almost every visible point.
Qualitative separation
(\Cref{def:duality_confinement:separating-readout}) identifies the
zero set of \(\psim\) with \(\Fix(J)\) on the visible support, so
the complement of \(\Fix(J)\) has \(\mu\)-measure zero.
\end{proof}

In the finite weighted case, the same proof says that every visible
object with positive weight lies in \(\Fix(J)\).

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative confinement\protect\footnote{Verified in Lean as \nolinkurl{DirectConfinement.quantitativeConfinement} via typed-bridge composition with a Markov-step hypothesis; see App~\ref{app:formalization}.}]
\label{thm:duality_confinement:quantitative-confinement}
Let \(\IOL\) be an involutive object ledger with anti-invariant
ledger \(\AX\) and quantitatively separating anti-invariant readout
\(\psim\) with modulus \(m\).  Then, for every
\(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
  \mu\bigl(\{x\in X:\dist(x,\Fix(J))\geq\varepsilon\}\bigr)
  \leq
  \frac{\trace\AX}{m(\varepsilon)^2}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(\varepsilon>0\), and set
\[
  E_\varepsilon=\{x\in X:\dist(x,\Fix(J))\geq\varepsilon\}.
\]
Quantitative separation gives
\[
  \|\psim(x)\|\geq m(\varepsilon)
  \qquad (x\in E_\varepsilon).
\]
A Markov-style integration step \citep{Folland1999} then yields
\[
  m(\varepsilon)^2\,\mu(E_\varepsilon)
  \leq
  \int_{E_\varepsilon}\|\psim(x)\|^2\,d\mu(x)
  \leq
  \int_X\|\psim(x)\|^2\,d\mu(x).
\]
The trace identity identifies the final integral with
\(\trace\AX\).  Since \(m(\varepsilon)>0\), division by
\(m(\varepsilon)^2\) gives the displayed bound.
\end{proof}

\Cref{sec:master_theorem} combines these direct consequences with
a domination squeeze: once a sequence of records
\(\AX\preceq B_n\) with \(\trace B_n\to0\) forces \(\AX=0\),
\Cref{thm:duality_confinement:separation-confinement} converts that
algebraic collapse into fixed-locus confinement.  The quantitative
bound above supplies the scale-sensitive refinement when one works
with finite-window or approximate data.
 

\section{Domination and Douglas factorization}
\label{sec:domination}

The direct confinement results of \Cref{sec:direct_confinement}
show what happens once \(\AX\) has vanished.  The master theorem
needs a way to force that vanishing from upper bounds whose trace
tends to zero, and domination records supply that input.  They
compare the anti-invariant ledger \(\AX\) to a positive
carrier-side quantity, denoted \(\Kminus\), together with an
explicit positive defect \(E\); the bridge formalism keeps the
useful carrier-side contribution and the residual error visible as
separate pieces.

\begin{definition}[Completed domination bridge]
\label{def:duality_confinement:completed-domination-bridge}
A \emph{completed domination bridge} from \(\AX\) to a
carrier-side anti-invariant currency \(\Kminus\) consists of a
positive defect \(E\) in the typed positive cone, with
\[
  0\preceq E,
\]
together with the domination relation
\[
  \AX\preceq \Kminus+E.
\]
The bridge is \emph{exact} when \(E=0\) and \emph{defected} when
the defect term is retained.  A sequence of bridges
\[
  \AX\preceq \Kminus_{n}+E_n
\]
is a \emph{domination-record sequence} when the associated positive
bounds
\[
  B_n=\Kminus_{n}+E_n
\]
satisfy
\[
  \trace B_n\to0
  \qquad (n\to\infty).
\]
\end{definition}

In ordinary operator language, the bridge says that the obstruction
\(\AX\) is controlled by something built on the carrier side, up to
a declared positive error.  The term \(\Kminus\) is the usable
anti-invariant currency: the part of the construction that an
application tries to make small by structural or analytic means.
The defect \(E\) records the part not absorbed into that currency.
If both terms can be arranged so that the trace of their sum tends
to zero along a sequence, trace monotonicity in the typed positive
cone gives the squeeze input needed later.

No proof is attached to the bridge itself; its content is the explicit
bookkeeping of the domination relation and the defect.
Proof work begins when one explains how such relations are
recognized or transported.  In the operator-theoretic setting used
as motivation here, exact bridges are represented through
operator-square-root data (see App.~\ref{app:formalization}); the
relevant factorization principle is Douglas factorization, taken
up next.

The factorization principle behind exact bridges is the classical
Douglas factorization \citep{Douglas1966}, traditionally stated as
a Hilbert-space operator equivalence.  In the present paper it is
used only through the typed-cone formulation needed by the bridge
apparatus.

\begin{theorem}[Douglas factorization, typed-cone form\protect\footnote{Verified in Lean as \nolinkurl{Domination.douglasDomination} via typed-carrier packaging in the \nolinkurl{DouglasData} structure; see App~\ref{app:formalization}.}]
\label{thm:duality_confinement:douglas-domination}
Let \(A=V^*V\) and \(K=W^*W\) be bounded positive operators on the
same response space, represented as positive elements of the typed
positive cone.  Then
\[
  A\preceq K
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \exists\,T\ \text{a contraction such that}\ V=TW.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In the classical operator-theoretic setting, this is Douglas's
factorization theorem: domination \(V^*V\preceq W^*W\) is equivalent
to the range-inclusion and norm-control condition that permits a
contractive factor \(T\) with \(V=TW\).  Conversely, if such a
contraction exists, then
\[
  V^*V = W^*T^*TW \preceq W^*W,
\]
because \(T^*T\preceq I\).

For the typed positive cone used in this paper, the equivalence and
its witness data are bundled into a typed carrier.  This records the
domination witness and the contractive-factor witness together, so
downstream arguments may pass between the two forms without
developing the full Hilbert-space proof inside the typed calculus.
\end{proof}
 

\section{The duality-confinement membrane theorem}
\label{sec:master_theorem}

This section states the main result: the duality-confinement
membrane theorem, also called the master theorem.  It takes the
apparatus built in the preceding sections and turns a sequence of
domination records into fixed-locus confinement.  The input is
algebraic and trace-theoretic---\(\AX\) is dominated by positive
bounds \(B_n\) whose traces tend to zero---and the output is
geometric: visible mass is confined to \(\Fix(J)\).  In framework
terms, this is the apparatus theorem for the SDTC structural law
\(\GamSDTC\): it does not derive the recognition source, only the
mathematical consequence once the source has supplied the required
sequence.

\begin{theorem}[Duality-confinement membrane theorem\protect\footnote{Verified in Lean as \nolinkurl{MasterTheorem.masterTheorem}; see App~\ref{app:formalization}.}]
\label{thm:duality_confinement:master-theorem}
Let \(\IOL\) be an involutive object ledger with carrier \(X\),
involution \(J\), measure \(\mu\), anti-invariant readout
\(\psim\), and anti-invariant ledger \(\AX\).  Assume that
\(\psim\) is qualitatively separating.  Suppose there is a sequence
of positive cone elements \(B_n\) such that
\[
  \AX\preceq B_n
  \qquad (n=1,2,\ldots)
\]
and
\[
  \trace B_n\to0
  \qquad (n\to\infty).
\]
Then \(\AX=0\) in the typed positive cone.  Consequently,
\[
  \mu\bigl(X\setminus\Fix(J)\bigr)=0.
\]
\end{theorem}

The proof is an operator-style squeeze, composing the apparatus of
\Cref{sec:anti_invariant_ledger,sec:direct_confinement,sec:domination}
with the direct-confinement consequence of
\Cref{thm:duality_confinement:separation-confinement}.

\begin{proof}
The argument has four steps.  First, trace monotonicity in the
typed positive cone turns each domination record into a scalar
inequality:
\[
  \AX\preceq B_n
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \trace\AX\leq\trace B_n.
\]
Since \(\AX\) is positive, \(\trace\AX\geq0\), so for every \(n\),
\[
  0\leq\trace\AX\leq\trace B_n.
\]

Second, the vanishing trace budget for the bounds gives the
squeeze.  The scalar quantity \(\trace\AX\) is independent of
\(n\), while \(\trace B_n\) tends to zero, so \(\trace\AX=0\).

Third, positivity-to-zero in the typed positive cone converts
trace-zero positivity into equality in the cone: \(\AX=0\).  This
is the point at which the proof uses the typed-cone abstraction
rather than a full trace-class operator theory.  In a classical
positive trace-class cone, the corresponding statement is that a
positive trace-class operator with trace zero is the zero operator.

Fourth, qualitative separation converts algebraic vanishing into
confinement.  With \(\AX=0\), the separation--confinement theorem
gives
\[
  \mu\bigl(X\setminus\Fix(J)\bigr)=0.
\]
That theorem in turn rests on the trace identity
\Cref{lem:duality_confinement:trace-identity}, which identifies
\(\trace\AX\) with the squared-norm integral of \(\psim\).
\end{proof}

The theorem is stated at the typed positive-cone level.  The cone
records exactly the primitives used by the proof: the order
\(\preceq\), the trace functional \(\trace\), trace monotonicity, and
the positivity-to-zero principle.  A fully operator-theoretic
treatment would require the trace-class topology, Hilbert--Schmidt
and Schatten-ideal infrastructure, and the corresponding analytic
closure lemmas \citep{Simon2005,ReedSimon1980}; that
operator-theoretic generality remains an open extension.

\paragraph{Worked single-substrate validation.}
The master theorem applies to any involutive object ledger whose
recognition source supplies the domination records required by the
squeeze hypothesis.  This paper develops the typed apparatus and
proves the squeeze.  A worked single-substrate validation,
instantiating the apparatus on the saturated completed Selberg trace
closure of the Riemann zeta function and applying the master theorem
to the corresponding zero ledger, is given in
\citet{TsiokosRHviaSDTC2026}.  That paper specifies the involution
\(J_L(s)=1-\bar{s}\), identifies its fixed locus with the critical
line, treats the saturated Selberg trace shell as the typed
substrate, and uses the Selberg-class instance of the SDTC
recognition source.  Its main theorem is conditional on the standard
Six Birds closure assumption and that Selberg-class instance of
SDTC; it imports the master theorem as stated in this section.  No
result from \citet{TsiokosRHviaSDTC2026} is used here.

The next section develops refinements, including exhaustive moving
ledgers and optimized scalar trace budgets, that adapt the master
theorem to finite-window and tail-defect situations.
 

\section{Exhaustive squeeze and defected budgets}
\label{sec:exhaustive_squeeze_and_budgets}

The master theorem is stated for ambient domination records
\(\AX\preceq B_n\) whose traces vanish.  In applications, the records
often arrive less directly: finite-window ledgers \(A_{X,n}\),
transports \(\iota_n\) into the completed ambient cone, and tails
\(T_n\) measuring what the window has not captured.  The exhaustive
moving ledger formalizes this finite-window-plus-tail situation.

\begin{definition}[Exhaustive moving ledger]
\label{def:duality_confinement:exhaustive-moving-ledger}
An \emph{exhaustive moving ledger} over an involutive object ledger
\(\IOL\) consists of a sequence of positive cone elements
\(A_{X,n}\), inclusion transports \(\iota_n\), and positive tails
\(T_n\) with \(0\preceq T_n\), such that, for every \(n\),
\[
  \AX\preceq \iota_n A_{X,n}\iota_n^*+T_n.
\]
It is \emph{vanishing-exhaustive} when there are positive bounds
\(B_n\) satisfying
\[
  A_{X,n}\preceq B_n
\]
and
\[
  \trace(\iota_n B_n\iota_n^*)+\trace T_n\to0
  \qquad (n\to\infty).
\]
\end{definition}

The finite-window term \(A_{X,n}\) approximates the completed ledger
\(\AX\); \(\iota_n\) transports it into the ambient cone, and \(T_n\)
records the residual positive contribution not captured by the
window.  The definition does not choose a particular analytic
truncation.  It isolates the trace-theoretic data needed for the
squeeze mechanism of \Cref{sec:master_theorem}:
the transported upper bound and the tail become invisible to the
trace in the limit.  This covers progressively finer finite windows,
increasing carrier subspaces, and approximation schemes where the
completed ledger is controlled by a transported finite piece plus a
trace-vanishing tail.

\begin{theorem}[Exhaustive ledger squeeze\protect\footnote{Verified in Lean as \nolinkurl{ExhaustiveSqueeze.exhaustiveSqueeze}; the ambient bound is supplied as a moving-ledger field. See App~\ref{app:formalization}.}]
\label{thm:duality_confinement:exhaustive-squeeze}
Let \(\IOL\) be an involutive object ledger with anti-invariant
ledger \(\AX\).  If \(\IOL\) admits a vanishing-exhaustive moving
ledger, then \(\AX=0\).  If, moreover, the anti-invariant readout
\(\psim\) is qualitatively separating, then
\[
  \mu\bigl(X\setminus\Fix(J)\bigr)=0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each \(n\), exhaustiveness gives
\[
  \AX\preceq \iota_n A_{X,n}\iota_n^*+T_n.
\]
The transported domination bound \(A_{X,n}\preceq B_n\) gives
\[
  \iota_n A_{X,n}\iota_n^*
  \preceq
  \iota_n B_n\iota_n^*.
\]
By trace monotonicity in the typed positive cone,
\[
  0\leq\trace\AX
  \leq
  \trace(\iota_n B_n\iota_n^*)+\trace T_n.
\]
The right-hand side tends to zero by vanishing-exhaustiveness, so
\(\trace\AX=0\).  Positivity-to-zero in the cone gives
\(\AX=0\).  If \(\psim\) is qualitatively separating, then
\Cref{thm:duality_confinement:separation-confinement} gives the
displayed fixed-locus confinement.
\end{proof}

The exhaustive squeeze covers records that vanish only after
transport and tail control.  The defected-budget refinement below
tracks two residual mechanisms---a source or membrane defect and a
bridge or domination defect---and re-weights them by a positive
scalar before minimizing the trace.

\begin{definition}[Defected obstruction budget]
\label{def:duality_confinement:defected-budget}
A \emph{defected obstruction budget} at level \(n\) is a domination
record of the form
\[
  \AX
  \preceq
  (1+t)\bigl(\Kminus_{n}+E_{\mathrm{src},n}\bigr)
  +(1+t^{-1})E_{\mathrm{br},n},
\]
where \(t>0\), where \(E_{\mathrm{src},n}\) is a source defect, and
where \(E_{\mathrm{br},n}\) is a bridge defect.  Both defects are
positive cone elements.  The unparameterized budget is obtained by
setting \(t=1\), which adds the two defect contributions without
optimization.
\end{definition}

The parameter \(t\) allocates weight between the source-side
contribution \(\Kminus_{n}+E_{\mathrm{src},n}\) and the bridge
defect \(E_{\mathrm{br},n}\); the reciprocal \(t^{-1}\) is the usual
two-term balancing factor.  The load-bearing scalar result is the
optimized trace estimate that follows.

\begin{proposition}[Optimized scalar trace budget\protect\footnote{Tracked in the formalization harness as \nolinkurl{DefectedBudget.optimizedTraceBudget}; the AM--GM step is supplied as a typed-scalar hypothesis. See App~\ref{app:formalization}.}]
\label{prop:duality_confinement:optimized-trace-budget}
For a defected obstruction budget at level \(n\), set
\[
  a_n=\trace(\Kminus_{n}+E_{\mathrm{src},n}),
  \qquad
  b_n=\trace E_{\mathrm{br},n},
\]
with \(a_n,b_n\geq0\).  Then
\[
  \inf_{t>0}
  \bigl[(1+t)a_n+(1+t^{-1})b_n\bigr]
  =
  \bigl(\sqrt{a_n}+\sqrt{b_n}\bigr)^2.
\]
In particular, if \(a_n\to0\) and \(b_n\to0\), then the optimized
trace budget tends to zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(a_n,b_n>0\), minimize
\[
  (1+t)a_n+(1+t^{-1})b_n
  =
  a_n+b_n+t a_n+\frac{b_n}{t}
\]
over \(t>0\).  The derivative vanishes at
\[
  t=\sqrt{\frac{b_n}{a_n}}.
\]
Substitution gives
\[
  a_n+b_n+2\sqrt{a_n b_n}
  =
  \bigl(\sqrt{a_n}+\sqrt{b_n}\bigr)^2.
\]
Equivalently, the AM--GM inequality
\citep{HardyLittlewoodPolya1952} applied to \(t a_n\) and
\(b_n/t\) gives
\[
  t a_n+\frac{b_n}{t}\geq2\sqrt{a_n b_n},
\]
with equality at the displayed value of \(t\).  If one of
\(a_n,b_n\) is zero, the same value is obtained as an infimum by
letting \(t\) tend to \(0\) or to \(+\infty\), as appropriate.
\end{proof}

named structural law.
 

\section{Scope and non-claims}
\label{sec:scope}

Several scope boundaries deserve explicit statement so the paper's
main claim remains sharp.  The point is not to weaken the apparatus
developed above, but to identify which structural content is being
invoked, which encoding choices are being made, and which
extensions remain outside the present paper.

\paragraph{SDTC is a named structural law, not a derived theorem.}
Self-Dual Trace Confinement is named as a recognition source:
structural content supplied by formed-layer closure per Foundations
I, not derived from framework primitives alone.  This paper does
not present SDTC as a theorem derived from earlier Six Birds
primitives.  The duality-confinement membrane theorem of
\Cref{sec:master_theorem} is the apparatus theorem proved here: it
takes domination records as input and extracts fixed-locus
confinement.  The load-bearing structural-law content is the
recognition source that supplies those records on a formed
self-dual layer.  The paper also establishes only the forward
apparatus implication from domination records to confinement; no
bidirectional equivalence between the SDTC structural claim and
the existence of those records is established here.

\paragraph{The theorem is at the typed-cone level.}
The master theorem is stated at the abstract positive-cone level with
an explicit trace functional, recording only the order \(\preceq\),
\(\trace\), trace monotonicity, and the positivity-to-zero principle.
Operator-theoretic generality---trace-class operators,
Hilbert--Schmidt operators, Schatten norms, trace-norm topology---is
not developed here as a full analytic model.  The classical
trace-class setting is the motivating model for the abstraction.

\paragraph{The scalar budget uses an AM--GM hypothesis.}
The AM--GM step in
\Cref{prop:duality_confinement:optimized-trace-budget} is the
classical inequality \citep{HardyLittlewoodPolya1952}.  In the
typed-scalar encoding used here, it is taken as a hypothesis on the
scalar interface; a derivation of AM--GM inside the abstract scalar
encoding would require additional typed real-arithmetic structure.
This is a scope statement on the typed-scalar abstraction, not a math
nonclaim about AM--GM itself.

\paragraph{Cross-substrate generalizations are structural predictions.}
The discussion of CPT symmetry, gauge invariance, Hermitian
conjugation, particle--antiparticle symmetry, quantum
self-adjointness, and function-field RH is predictive rather than
theorem-level.  These are structural predictions that genuine
involutive self-duality at the formed-layer level implies
fixed-locus confinement; their plausibility comes from consistency
with observed physical and mathematical symmetries.  They are not
established results in this paper, and each remains a
single-substrate validation problem outside the abstract apparatus
developed here.

\paragraph{No novelty is claimed for standard operator theory.}
Douglas factorization \citep{Douglas1966}, trace functionals
\citep{Simon2005}, Loewner-style order
\citep{Loewner1934,Bhatia1997,HornJohnson2013}, Markov-type
estimates \citep{Folland1999}, AM--GM
\citep{HardyLittlewoodPolya1952}, and standard rank-one trace
identities \citep{ReedSimon1980} are treated as classical
mathematical constructions; the present paper does not rebrand
those results as new.  The
contribution is the structural packaging: the involutive object
ledger, the anti-invariant ledger, the completed-domination bridge,
the exhaustive moving ledger, and the master theorem that converts
vanishing trace budgets into fixed-locus confinement.  Classical
operator-theoretic references are background for the operator
model, not novelty claims of this paper.

These boundaries identify which framework content is supplied as
recognition source, which typed abstractions are used here, and what
would count as an extension of the present result.  \(\GamSDTC\) is
the named structural law, and the master theorem is its apparatus
theorem.
 

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The duality-confinement membrane theorem suggests three lines of
further thought: a common structural pattern behind several
self-dual settings, the relation between the typed-cone squeeze and
standard operator-theoretic squeeze arguments, and the extensions
needed to move from the present apparatus to broader
single-substrate validations.

\paragraph{Cross-substrate structural pointers.}
The examples below are structural predictions of the
\(\GamSDTC\) framing, not established theorems of this paper.  The
common template is conditional: if a formed closure carries a
genuine involutive self-duality on its visible ledger, then the
master theorem's hypotheses would apply once the corresponding
separating readout and domination-record discipline are supplied.

For quantum self-adjointness, the candidate involution is Hermitian
conjugation \(T\mapsto T^*\) on an operator ledger
\citep{ReedSimon1975,Hall2013}.  The fixed locus is the self-adjoint
part \(T=T^*\), and the natural anti-invariant readout is the
skew-Hermitian component.  The required single-substrate validation
would show that this skew component is separating and that the
formed quantum closure supplies trace-vanishing domination records
for it.

For CPT symmetry, the candidate involution is the CPT operation on
field or state data \citep{StreaterWightman2000,Weinberg1995}.  The
fixed locus is the CPT-invariant sector, and the readout is the
CPT-anti-invariant component.  The structural prediction is that a
CPT-genuine formed layer cannot retain visible CPT-odd mass once
the domination records are available.  This is a pointer toward the
shape of a validation problem, not a derivation of CPT invariance.

For gauge invariance, the relevant involution would have to be an
order-two gauge operation, gauge-duality, or gauge reflection
realized on the visible ledger of the substrate
\citep{PeskinSchroeder1995,Bleecker1981}.  The fixed locus would be
the gauge-invariant observables, while the readout would record
gauge-variant components.  The master theorem would then ask for
the same three ingredients as before: an involutive object ledger,
a separating anti-invariant readout, and a trace-vanishing
domination sequence.

For particle--antiparticle symmetry, charge conjugation is the
candidate involution \citep{PeskinSchroeder1995,Weinberg1995}.  The
fixed locus consists of self-conjugate states or charge-neutral
visible configurations, and the anti-invariant readout measures the
matter--antimatter asymmetry coordinate.  The prediction is again
structural: confinement to the fixed locus would follow only after
the substrate-specific recognition source supplies the domination
records.

For function-field RH, the candidate involution is the
Frobenius-functional-equation involution for the relevant
function-field \(L\)-function \citep{Weil1948a,Weil1948b,Stepanov1969,Bombieri1974}.
The fixed locus is the analogue of the critical line in the
Frobenius eigenvalue plane, and the anti-invariant readout is the
corresponding off-line displacement.  This is closest in shape to
the Riemann-zero ledger used above as a motivating example, but it
still requires its own formed-layer ledger, separation proof, and
recognition source.

These five examples share a shape but not a proof.  Each would
require a separate validation establishing the involution and
fixed locus as data of a formed-layer involutive object ledger,
proving the readout's separating property, and supplying the
domination records required by the master theorem.  Until those
pieces are in place, the examples remain pointers for
cross-substrate applicability.

\paragraph{Relation to classical operator theory.}
Outside the Six Birds framing, the master theorem reads as a typed
abstraction of a classical operator-theoretic fact: a positive
operator dominated by a sequence of positive operators whose traces
tend to zero must itself vanish.  In the concrete positive
trace-class cone, this follows from the Loewner order, trace
monotonicity, and positivity of the trace.  Standard operator-theory
and matrix-analysis references
\citep{ReedSimon1980,Simon2005,Bhatia1997,HornJohnson2013} provide
the classical ambient language for such facts.

The present paper shares the spirit of that operator theory but is
structurally distinct from it.  The typed positive cone is an
abstraction of the order, trace, and positivity principles used by
the proof; it is not a development of the trace-norm topology or
Schatten-ideal infrastructure.  The additional Six Birds content is
not the scalar squeeze itself but the structural-law framing: the
trace-vanishing sequence is supplied as recognition-source content
of a formed self-dual closure, and the apparatus theorem turns
that content into fixed-locus confinement.

\paragraph{Future work.}
The first extension target is an operator-theoretic realization of
the typed cone.  One would instantiate the cone with positive
trace-class operators on a Hilbert space, prove the trace identity
and positivity-to-zero inside that setting, and connect the
Douglas factorization carrier used here to the usual range
inclusion theorem for bounded Hilbert-space operators.

The second extension target is scalar arithmetic.  The optimized
trace budget currently tracks the AM--GM step through a
typed-scalar hypothesis.  A stronger treatment would introduce
enough typed real-arithmetic structure to express
\[
  \bigl(\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{b}\bigr)^2\geq0
\]
and derive AM--GM internally rather than using it as a supplied
scalar fact.

The third extension target is additional single-substrate
validation.  The Riemann-zero ledger is the guiding analytic
instance; the structural pointers above suggest other candidates,
but each must be developed on its own terms.  A natural sequence
would begin with function-field RH and then proceed to quantum
self-adjointness or charge-conjugation settings, where the
involution and fixed locus are comparatively explicit.

Read outside the Six Birds framing, the master theorem is a
typed-cone squeeze theorem abstracting a familiar positive-operator
argument; the additional structural-law content of SDTC is the
claim that, in formed self-dual closures, the trace-vanishing
domination sequence is supplied as closure content rather than
derived from the readout alone.  Once such a sequence is in place,
the apparatus does one thing: it converts the trace-vanishing
content into confinement on \(\Fix(J)\).
 

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper names \(\GamSDTC\) as the structural law for formed
closures with genuine involutive self-duality: visible mass is
confined to the fixed locus of the duality.  Its apparatus is the
duality-confinement membrane theorem, which converts a
vanishing-trace domination sequence
\[
  \AX\preceq B_n,
  \qquad
  \trace B_n\to0,
\]
into \(\AX=0\), hence confinement on \(\Fix(J)\).  The body
develops the corresponding typed machinery: involutive object
ledgers, anti-invariant ledgers with trace identity and separating
readout, completed-domination bridges with typed-cone Douglas
factorization, exhaustive moving ledgers, and optimized scalar
trace budgets.  The development is formalized in Lean as a
mathlib-free mechanization at the typed positive-cone level
(Appendix~\ref{app:formalization}).

Two apparatus-layer extensions remain open.  First, the typed positive cone
should be realized as the full positive trace-class cone on a
Hilbert space, with the trace-norm topology, Hilbert--Schmidt and
Schatten infrastructure, and the classical operator-theoretic
lemmas made explicit \citep{Simon2005,ReedSimon1980}.  Second, the
optimized scalar trace budget should be strengthened by deriving
AM--GM \citep{HardyLittlewoodPolya1952} inside a typed
real-arithmetic structure rather than taking the inequality as a
scalar hypothesis.

No single-substrate validation is used here.  The present paper is
the abstract apparatus layer; substrate-specific applications must
supply their own involutive ledger, separating readout, and
recognition source.

Code and data availability.  The source repository is available at
\url{https://github.com/ioannist/six-birds-duality-confinement}.
The math source-of-record for the paper's claims is the artifact at
\path{anti_loc/extracted_math/duality_confinement_master.md} within
that repository.  The per-paper manifest is at
\path{lean/manifests/duality_confinement_manifest.toml}.

\subsection*{Typed positive-cone encoding}

The formalization uses a mathlib-free typed positive cone for \(\AX\),
the bounds \(B_n\), the carrier currency \(\Kminus\), and the tails
\(T_n\).  The cone records exactly the primitives used in the paper:

at this level: the Gram-type construction of \(\AX\) is bundled with
the assertion that \(\trace\AX\) is the integral of

This is narrower than full Hilbert-space operator theory.  The master
theorem uses only order, trace monotonicity, scalar squeezing, and
positivity-to-zero; it does not formalize trace-class operators,
Hilbert-Schmidt maps, Schatten ideals, or trace-norm topology
\citep{Simon2005,ReedSimon1980}.  Realizing the theorem in the full
positive trace-class cone is an operator-theoretic extension, not a
hidden Lean claim.

\subsection*{Representation notes}

\paragraph{Douglas factorization.}
The classical mathematical content here is Douglas factorization
\citep{Douglas1966}: for bounded positive operators \(A=V^*V\) and
\(K=W^*W\) on a Hilbert space, domination \(A\preceq K\) is
equivalent to the existence of a contraction \(T\) with \(V=TW\).
The domination section uses this as operator-theoretic motivation.

In the mathlib-free typed-cone encoding this theorem is represented
by a typed \texttt{DouglasData} carrier.  The carrier records
\(A=V^*V\), \(K=W^*W\), the domination relation \(A\preceq K\), the
contractive factor witness \(T\) with \(V=TW\), and the two fields
\texttt{order\_to\_factor} and \texttt{factor\_to\_order}.  Downstream
theorems read the domination--factorization implication from these
fields.

Thus \texttt{douglasDomination} repackages stored carrier fields into
the
\(\mathtt{Nonempty\ Domination}\leftrightarrow\mathtt{Nonempty\ ContractiveFactor}\)
form used by the bridge apparatus.  This is typed-carrier packaging,
not a Lean development of the full Hilbert-space theorem from
operator-square-root primitives.

\paragraph{AM--GM in the scalar trace budget.}
The optimized scalar trace budget depends on the elementary
optimization
\[
  \inf_{t>0}\bigl((1+t)a+(1+t^{-1})b\bigr)
    = \bigl(\sqrt a+\sqrt b\bigr)^2 .
\]
In ordinary real arithmetic this follows from the AM--GM inequality
\citep{HardyLittlewoodPolya1952}, or equivalently from
non-negativity of \((\sqrt a-\sqrt b)^2\).  The abstract scalar
encoding used here is weaker: it provides the scalar interface
needed by the trace-budget statement but does not supply enough
ordered real arithmetic to derive AM--GM substantively.

Accordingly, the Lean-side statement uses an explicit
\texttt{am\_gm\_optimization} hypothesis.  The optimized scalar trace
budget is tracked as a typed projection over that hypothesis, not as a
substantive Lean derivation of AM--GM.  A future strengthening would
add typed real-arithmetic structure for square roots, non-negativity
of squares, and the derivation of \((\sqrt a-\sqrt b)^2\geq0\).

\paragraph{Anti-invariant readout as typed field.}
The body defines \(\psim(x)=\Pminus\psi(x)\), and equivariance of
\(\psi\) forces \(\psim(Jx)=-\psim(x)\).  The mathlib-free typed-cone
encoding does not represent \(\Pminus\), so \(\psim\) is an
independent typed field of the separating-readout and
anti-invariant-ledger carriers.  A coherence hypothesis identifies the
two occurrences pointwise, and downstream theorems consume \(\psim\)
through these fields.

The projection identity and anti-equivariance are therefore paper-side
mathematics, not derived inside Lean from the ledger's \(\psi\) and
\(\Jiso\) fields.  A future strengthening would add a response-side
\(\Pminus\) primitive, or an anti-action on the readout codomain, so
that the projection and equivariance identities can be derived rather
than supplied as typed-interface data.
