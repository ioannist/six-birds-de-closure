# 11. Limits and open problems

## 11.1 Limits of the results

The models are small and declared. The allocation model has 32 patches and a sum budget; the record window uses linear
growth, a fixed collapse threshold and an imposed erasure rule; the lattice model has eight sites. Their theorems hold for
these models. The orbit classification covers the sampled offsets and couplings, not every intermediate value. Several
statements are certified by exact computations outside Lean, and the sampled sweeps, measure tables and audit fits are
floating-point results without rigorous error bounds. The staging audit is calibrated with twenty mocks at a fitted
anchor, so its false-alarm level is not guaranteed, and its detection rates are empirical. Results imported from other Six
Birds papers frame the questions but are not proved here.

## 11.2 What would change the picture

| development | what it would settle |
|---|---|
| a map from a closure budget to a gravitational field equation, with conserved stress-energy and the right dimensions and sign | whether the price of Section 4 can be the physical cosmological constant |
| a selection rule | a parameter choice for the value question of Section 6, within the models |
| a physically motivated prior with quantitative bounds over the whole window | a typicality calculation for Section 7, within the models; a prior alone does not fix the realized value |
| staging or probe dependence of the inferred correction that survives a composite-null calibration and grows with lever arm | it would establish, or its absence would constrain, calibrated inconsistency within the specified likelihood and nuisance model; distinguishing physical origins needs independently specified competing models and predictions |
| one growth data set spanning slack and strong shells under a common covariance, with validated nonlinear modeling | the decisive within-data-set growth test of Section 8.5, in either direction |
| a stable fundamental model that produces the fitted crossing history | a stable physical realization of that history; distinguishing among realizations would need further predictions and observations |

## 11.3 Open mathematical problems

Three problems are natural next steps. First, classify the orbits of the lattice model on whole intervals of offset and
coupling, including the transition between convergence and two-cycles between $\kappa=15$ and $\kappa=20$. Second,
mechanize the global existence of the constrained ledger flow of Section 6.4, which is currently a paper-level
argument. Third, give the staging audit a calibration with a guaranteed level for a composite null whose nuisance
parameters are fitted to the data.
