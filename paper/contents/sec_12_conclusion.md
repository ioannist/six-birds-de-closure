# 12. Conclusion

We separated the dark-energy problem into questions of different kinds and posed each to a finite model or a data audit.
In the models, a strictly sensitive global budget carries one positive price, and, for an originally binding budget, a common
linear term shifts that price without moving the allocation as long as the shifted price stays nonnegative; in a toy universe, at least ten of twelve seeds keep records exactly for
$-1/8000<\lambda\le\lambda_+$ with $3<\lambda_+<3.4$, without pinning the coincidence ratio; the convergence readouts of a
self-consistent lattice model do not determine its background offset, and a global ledger turns the absolute value into a
gauge coordinate that no record-only selector can track and that carries no translation-invariant probability; and none
of five tested priors meets the concentration criterion, while smoothness near zero cannot ensure it. In the data, a rewrite family is constant-compatible, the best-fit evolving history has a regular
density through its phantom crossing, a staging audit flags structured dependence between supernova and BAO subsets, and
no single surveyed growth probe can yet host the decisive test.

These results make "contingent modulus" a precise, model-relative statement and show what a selection principle or a
prior would have to supply. They do not identify the model price with the physical cosmological constant, do not derive
its value, and do not refute a fundamental $\Lambda$. The statements labelled as Lean theorems are kernel-checked, and
the local numerical results are documented by committed artifacts and reproduction scripts in the accompanying
repository.
