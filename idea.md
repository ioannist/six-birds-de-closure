## State of play as of **May 29, 2026**

The live center of the dark-energy debate is **DESI DR2**. DESI’s three-year BAO release, using more than **14 million galaxies and quasars**, reports that flat ΛCDM still fits many observables, but combined DESI BAO + CMB + supernova analyses prefer a time-varying dark-energy equation of state at roughly **3.1σ** for DESI BAO+CMB and **2.8–4.2σ** when supernova compilations are added, depending on the SN dataset. The crucial caveat is that this is not yet a clean discovery of new physics: several follow-up papers find dataset, calibration, likelihood, or model-selection subtleties that weaken or reinterpret the signal. ([arXiv][1])

My bottom-line reading: **ΛCDM is under pressure, but the strongest “crisis” language is not yet warranted across all nine issues.** The sharpest current observational issues are DESI’s possible evolving (w(z)), the Hubble tension, and the dataset-consistency question. The sharpest theory issues are the cosmological-constant problem and the phantom-crossing/ghost problem.

---

## 1. Cosmological constant problem

**What is still open:** This remains the deepest theoretical problem: observed dark-energy density is extraordinarily tiny relative to naive quantum-vacuum estimates. None of the 2025–2026 work I found gives a community-accepted solution; the field is still exploring mechanisms rather than converging on one.

**Recent work:** Khoury, Muntz & Padilla proposed a 2026 “lapse”/higher-dimensional mechanism related to vacuum-energy sequestering, in which global constraints cancel Standard Model radiative vacuum-energy contributions “at all orders” in their effective setup. That is interesting because it targets radiative stability, not merely the small observed value, but it is still a theoretical construction rather than an empirically selected model. ([arXiv][2])

A related 2026 paper by van de Bruck, Burgess & Smith argues that dynamical vacuum-energy relaxation mechanisms can have nontrivial low-energy consequences; in their “yoga” relaxation framework, conventional QCD axions become difficult to keep viable in their standard form. That matters because proposed solutions to the cosmological-constant problem can collide with otherwise attractive particle-physics solutions. ([arXiv][3])

**DESI connection:** Yin’s JHEP paper argues that a scalar field scanning the cosmological constant can also reproduce the kind of time-varying dark energy hinted by early DESI results, so DESI has motivated attempts to connect the old cosmological-constant problem with new (w(z)) anomalies. But this is not a solution endorsed by observation; it is a model-building bridge. ([Springer][4])

## 2. Hubble tension

**Current finding:** The tension remains real, but its severity depends on which late-universe distance ladder one trusts. The early-universe Planck ΛCDM inference gives (H_0 = 67.4 \pm 0.5\ \mathrm{km,s^{-1},Mpc^{-1}}). ([arXiv][5])

On the high-(H_0) side, SH0ES/JWST Cepheid work reports that JWST agrees with HST Cepheid distances and supports (H_0) values around 72–73; Riess et al. report (H_0 = 73.18 \pm 0.88) when combining JWST Cepheids with HST and TRGB calibration, about (6\sigma) above the CMB/ΛCDM expectation. ([ui.adsabs.harvard.edu][6])

On the lower-(H_0) side, the Carnegie–Chicago Hubble Program’s JWST analysis using TRGB/JAGB/Cepheid calibrators finds values closer to the CMB expectation: TRGB (H_0 = 70.39 \pm 1.22_{\rm stat} \pm 1.33_{\rm sys} \pm 0.70_{\rm SN}), JWST-only TRGB (H_0 = 68.81), and JAGB (H_0 = 67.80) with larger uncertainties. ([arXiv][7])

**Interpretation:** The Hubble tension is not solved by DESI-style evolving dark energy. In fact, some DESI (w_0w_a) fits lower the inferred (H_0), worsening the mismatch with SH0ES. New interacting-dark-energy models can raise (H_0), but at the price of extra assumptions and parameter freedom. ([arXiv][8])

## 3. Dataset inconsistencies and distance-duality claims

**Your strongest claim needs nuance.** The paper you cited, Afroz & Mukherjee’s arXiv:2504.16868, indeed argues that Pantheon+ supernovae and DESI DR2 BAO show a redshift-dependent violation of the cosmic distance duality relation, and that this can mimic evolving dark energy when the datasets are combined. In their treatment, accounting for the effect weakens or removes the DESI evolving-dark-energy claim, yielding (w_0=-0.92\pm0.08) and (w_a\approx -0.49) with broad uncertainty. ([arXiv][9])

But several newer analyses push back. A 2026 model-independent consistency test using Crossing Statistics finds that DESI DR2 BAO and Pantheon+/Union3 supernovae remain mutually consistent while still allowing a low-redshift evolving-dark-energy reconstruction. ([arXiv][10])

Another 2026 analysis using PAge and Gaussian-process methods finds cosmic distance duality valid within (1\sigma) using recent SN, BAO, chronometer, and GRB data, including DESI DR2. ([arXiv][11])

A DESI–DES supernova cross-check finds the transverse BAO distance (D_M/r_d) consistent with supernova distances up to calibration, while the radial (D_H/r_d) part shows only a mild trend whose significance drops depending on statistical treatment and the DES-Dovekie update. ([arXiv][12])

**Interpretation:** There is a real consistency debate, but it is not settled that BAO and supernovae are “mathematically inconsistent.” The more precise statement is: **some combinations and calibrations can mimic distance-duality violation or evolving (w(z)), while other model-independent tests find consistency.**

## 4. Phantom crossing and ghost instability

**The observational issue:** DESI’s extended dark-energy analysis explicitly reports that the data prefer a (w(z)) history crossing the phantom divide (w=-1), especially at low redshift, although alternatives without crossing are “disfavored but not ruled out.” ([arXiv][13])

**The theory issue:** A fundamental single scalar field with (w<-1) usually implies a wrong-sign kinetic term, i.e., a ghost instability. Recent papers continue to treat this as a serious constraint: non-phantom quintessence remains theoretically cleaner, while true phantom theories risk vacuum instabilities, ghosts, Lorentz problems, or related pathologies. ([arXiv][14])

**Recent nuance:** The crossing may be *effective* rather than fundamental. Antusch, King & Wang show that coupled dark energy and dark matter can produce an apparent phantom crossing without introducing a phantom scalar; the catch is that the scalar field must be frozen early enough to avoid CMB constraints and evolve strongly enough late to match DESI-like behavior. ([arXiv][15])

Keeley, Shafieloo & Matthewson ran simulations showing that even non-phantom fiducial models can occasionally produce apparent CPL phantom-crossing fits; they estimate this happens in about **3.2%** of their mock cases, so evolving dark energy may be more robust than the exact crossing. ([arXiv][16])

Dinda, Maartens & Saito argue from Bayesian model comparison that local goodness-of-fit improvements do not provide statistically consistent evidence for either dynamical dark energy or phantom crossing across datasets. ([arXiv][17])

**Interpretation:** The “phantom crossing crisis” is real if DESI is pointing to a fundamental (w<-1) field. It is less fatal if the crossing is an effective description caused by modified gravity, dark-sector interaction, multi-field dynamics, or distance-calibration artifacts.

## 5. (S_8) / growth-of-structure tension

**Current finding:** The (S_8) tension has softened compared with the strongest earlier claims, but it has not disappeared in every dataset. DES Year 6 cosmic shear, using the full six-year DES sample, finds (S_8=0.789\pm0.012), about **1.8σ** from a combined Planck+ACT+SPT CMB comparison. ([arXiv][18])

KiDS-Legacy moves in the other direction: its latest cosmic-shear analysis reports (S_8=0.815^{+0.016}_{-0.021}), only about **0.73σ** from Planck, after improved redshift calibration and simulations. ([arXiv][19])

A 2025/2026 HSC-Y3 reanalysis using DESI redshift calibration also shifts (S_8) upward, from the earlier HSC-Y3 (S_8\approx0.769) to (S_8=0.805\pm0.018), and a combined HSC+KiDS+DES analysis gives (S_8\approx0.813), broadly consistent with CMB values. ([arXiv][20])

**Interpretation:** The old slogan “weak lensing says the universe is too smooth” is now probe-dependent. DES Y6 remains somewhat low, but KiDS/HSC recalibrations reduce the global tension. The emerging issue is less “ΛCDM definitely predicts too much clumpiness” and more “different low-redshift structure probes are sensitive to calibration, baryonic physics, intrinsic alignments, and redshift-systematics choices.”

## 6. Coincidence problem

**What remains open:** This is still a conceptual/theory problem, not a direct observational anomaly. ΛCDM says matter density falls as the universe expands while vacuum energy stays constant, so the near-equality of matter and dark energy today is unexplained.

**Recent directions:** Interacting dark energy has long been attractive because energy exchange can create scaling solutions or delay coincidence. A 2026 paper introduces a late-time threshold model where dark-sector interaction turns on only below a transition redshift; it finds low-redshift activation, energy flow from dark energy to dark matter, and fits comparable to CPL without phantom crossing. ([arXiv][21])

A very recent May 2026 paper proposes an Einstein–Cartan torsion mechanism in non-interacting holographic dark energy: torsion makes the matter/dark-energy density ratio dynamical even without a phenomenological interaction (Q), allows acceleration, and can realize an order-unity density ratio in a weak-torsion regime. ([arXiv][22])

Holographic dark-energy models are often advertised as addressing both the cosmological-constant and coincidence problems, but 2025–2026 ACT+DESI analyses also stress observational challenges for holographic and Ricci dark-energy paradigms. ([arXiv][23])

**Interpretation:** The coincidence problem has many proposed dynamical “resolutions,” but none is observationally compelled. DESI’s possible evolving (w(z)) makes these models more interesting, not confirmed.

## 7. Interacting dark sector

**Current observational situation:** Interacting dark-energy models are being actively revisited because they can mimic evolving (w(z)), soften (H_0), and sometimes affect (S_8). But the latest results are mixed.

Pan et al. test interacting dark energy against Planck 2018, DESI DR2 BAO, cosmic chronometers, and supernova samples; they find a mild interaction preference slightly above (1\sigma), still within (2\sigma), and model comparison gives a mixed picture: some criteria prefer interaction, while Bayesian/AIC-style comparisons can favor ΛCDM. ([arXiv][24])

Silva et al. find that DESI DR2 constraints on traditional and sign-switching interacting dark-energy models can reduce the (H_0) tension to about **2.7σ**, with the standard IDE model compatible with current (S_8) constraints and sign-switching IDE giving lower (S_8); they report only mild evidence for a nonzero coupling. ([arXiv][25])

A more aggressive 2025 interacting model claims (H_0=71.90\pm1.00) and reduces the (H_0) tension to **0.8σ** when SH0ES is included, but that success comes from introducing a dynamical cosmological-constant scale parameter and should be read as model-dependent rather than a generic resolution. ([arXiv][26])

**Interpretation:** Interacting dark sectors are among the most flexible ways to address several tensions at once. The conundrum is exactly what you wrote: they replace one problem with new microphysics, new couplings, and degeneracies. Current data allow mild hints, not a robust discovery of a dark-sector force.

## 8. Modified gravity vs. dark energy

**Latest gravity tests:** DESI’s full-shape clustering analysis tests deviations from GR using growth-of-structure information. DESI DR1 full-shape + BAO combined with CMB and DESY3 gives modified-gravity parameters consistent with general relativity, e.g. (\mu_0=0.04\pm0.22) and (\Sigma_0=0.044\pm0.047). ([arXiv][27])

A dedicated DESI modified-gravity paper likewise finds all tested modified-gravity parameters consistent with GR, while still recovering a preference for dynamical dark energy in (w_0w_a) backgrounds. ([arXiv][28])

A 2026 scale-dependent modified-gravity analysis using DESI DR1 finds no evidence for deviations from GR and constrains Hu–Sawicki-like (f(R)) gravity with (\log_{10}|f_{R0}|<-4.59) at 95% confidence, corresponding to a fifth-force range below about 17.8 Mpc. ([arXiv][29])

**But modified gravity remains alive:** Scalar-tensor reconstructions can naturally allow effective phantom crossing and can raise inferred (H_0); Efstratiou & collaborators find (H_0=70.6\pm1.7) in one such DESI DR2 + Pantheon+ + CMB + RSD framework. ([arXiv][8])

**Interpretation:** The strongest current statement is: **large-scale data do not demand modified gravity, but modified gravity remains a viable interpretation of effective evolving dark energy if it passes growth, lensing, Solar-System, and gravitational-wave constraints.**

## 9. Anthropic principle / multiverse retreat

**Current status:** Anthropic reasoning remains an active fallback, not an experimentally confirmed explanation. Recent theory is not just hand-waving; it tries to make anthropic arguments more concrete.

A 2025/2026 axion + negative cosmological-constant model studies a string-axiverse-motivated setup in which dark energy is a bare negative (\Lambda) plus an ultralight axion. Under restricted anthropic conditions—observers see acceleration after matter domination—the model derives nontrivial constraints on the axion mass and bare (\Lambda), and predicts deviations of (w) from (-1) that could be relevant to DESI-like hints. ([arXiv][30])

**Interpretation:** The anthropic approach has not gone away, but DESI has changed the framing. If (w=-1) is exactly true, anthropic/landscape explanations of a tiny constant (\Lambda) remain tempting. If DESI’s evolving (w(z)) survives, then a pure cosmological constant becomes less likely, and anthropic models must explain not just “why this tiny value?” but “why this time-dependent history?”

## 1. The central paper strategy

The precursor dark-energy paper already has the right seed: cosmological inference is a package

[
\mathcal{T}=(X,T,{f_p},U,\mathcal{A}),
]

where (X) is the underlying world-state, (T) is evolution, (f_p) are probe lenses — CMB, BAO, SN, weak lensing, RSD, local distance ladder — (U) is the homogeneous FLRW completion, and (\mathcal{A}) is the audit system. The dark-energy term is then not assumed to be a substance; it is the **least rewrite** needed when

[
U f_p T \neq T_{\rm FLRW} U f_p.
]

The new paper should sharpen this into a named object:

[
\Xi_{\rm cosmo}(z,\ell,p)
]

the **cosmological closure residual**, indexed by redshift (z), scale or staging (\ell), and probe (p). Then the whole paper argues that the major dark-energy paradoxes are different projections of (\Xi_{\rm cosmo}).

This is especially timely because DESI DR2 reports that (w_0w_a)CDM gives a better fit than (\Lambda)CDM, with DESI BAO+CMB preferring dynamical dark energy at (3.1\sigma), and DESI+SN combinations giving (2.8)–(4.2\sigma), depending on the SN sample. ([arXiv][1]) DESI’s extended analysis also says the data prefer models with phantom crossing, though non-crossing alternatives are not ruled out. ([arXiv][2]) That is exactly where Six Birds can become useful: it can say, “the effective (w(z)) is not the primitive object; it is a readout of a closure repair.”

## 2. What to keep and what to upgrade from the precursor

Keep the precursor’s core framing:

[
E = U\circ f,\qquad
{\rm RM}(x)=d(E T x, T E x).
]

That is the heart of the argument. Also keep the toy result where a null-(\Lambda), heterogeneous generator is fit by homogeneous (\Lambda)CDM and returns an apparent positive (\Omega_\Lambda). That is the cleanest “Six Birds mechanism demo.”

But the current version is not yet strong enough to settle the modern dark-energy problem. It needs four upgrades.

First, move from DES-only public probes to **DESI DR2 as the central battleground**. The paper should reproduce the DESI DR2 (w_0w_a) signal before introducing Six Birds. Otherwise reviewers will not trust the new machinery.

Second, replace the surrogate LSS backend with a real physical likelihood or a clearly staged two-paper plan. The (S_8) claim cannot be convincing using only a surrogate backend. Third, put the distance-duality controversy at the center. Afroz and Mukherjee argue that Pantheon+ SN and DESI DR2 BAO violate cosmic distance duality and that, once this inconsistency is included, the DESI evolving-dark-energy signal is absent. ([arXiv][4]) But a 2026 Crossing Statistics analysis finds DESI DR2 BAO and Pantheon+/Union3 remain mutually consistent under model-independent deformations and still suggest low-redshift evolving dark energy. ([arXiv][5]) This is almost tailor-made for Six Birds: it is a **local-global gluing problem**.

Fourth, use the foundations papers more aggressively. The precursor mostly uses lens/completion/mismatch. The new paper should also use:

* **AOR:** the paper should not merely present fits; it should present a closure-completed audit carrier.
